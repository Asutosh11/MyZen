🧘 The Main Daily Practice

When sitting for your main daily session, follow these 4 simple steps to quiet the mind:

1
Prepare the Body
Do some exercise, stretching or movement. Make it a timetable everyday. So that your body can sit upright and still without restlessness and fatigue. When beginning a session, find a comfortable place, on a chair or on the floor with a mat, and sit straight.
2
Observe Natural Breath
Sit comfortably, close your eyes, and watch your breath flow naturally without forcing it. At first the breath will be fast paced, which will slow down in a few mins. When mind drifts away from thoughts, bring attention back to breath as soon as you notice
3
Anchor Focus Inward
After a few mins, breath will slow down, its not racing anymore. After few more mins, breath will be felt at the nose and then at the point between the eyebrows. When breath becomes very very slow, forget the breath and just focus & feel the point at the space between your eyebrows (Kūṭastha). Gently return focus here if thoughts wander.
4
Rest in Stillness
Remain aware. If mind gets drifted away with thoughts, bring the attention back to the point between the eyebrows as soon as you notice. When ending the session, open the eyes gently. Be in the same position for a few moment, feel the changes, before getting up.

How effectiveness of a meditation session be measured?

Using Patanjali's 8 Limbs

What are the Patanjali's 8 Limbs? Tracking sit time is one thing, but depth means far more in meditation than sit time length. Patanjali's 8 Limbs serve as a subjective internal ruler for attention depth. Each limb includes its Theory (Sutra background), What It Means (practical definition), and How a Sadhak Feels in Reality.

1/2. Yama/Niyama - restraints & discipline. 3. Asana - steady posture. 4. Pranayama - breath integration. 5. Pratyahara - sensory withdrawl. 6. Dharana - one pointed focus. 7. Dhyana - effortless joy. 8. Samadhi - oneness.

How they measure effectiveness:

While clock duration tracks physical effort, Patanjali's 8 Limbs serve as an internal ruler for quality. By logging both Time (duration in minutes) and Highest Limb Touched (depth 1–8), you measure both time length and the far more vital dimension: true internal depth of attention. In every session, the depth achieved means far more than time spent.

Higher on the tree = deeper meditation
A 10-minute sit touching Dhyana is deeper than a 60-minute sit stuck at Pranayama.

🌐 What & How The Web App Works

MyZen is a minimalist meditation journal that measures depth, not just duration. The web app acts as your live personal reflection dashboard. Anyone can view the overall depth philosophy, but when you log in, it becomes your private interactive meditation journal.

⚡ GitHub Sync & Data Ownership

Your entries are saved to sessions.json in your own Github repository. The Login process creates a public Github repo in your own account, if it doesn't exist. Else uses existing. By signing in with a Personal Access Token, updates commit straight to your own GitHub via the REST API - no middleman servers.

Why I Built This

Meditation is deeply personal.

Most apps focus on productivity metrics—minutes, streaks, badges, and achievements.

I wanted something quieter.

A journal that simply reflects practice over time. To motivate me to maintain consistency.

The Goal of meditation is to finally reach Samadhi, and during the process you keep changing as a person. Before reaching that ultimate state, there are intermediate states, which also need constant practice to reach and I want to document my entire journey.
